HYGROSTREAM

Optimus Beast Flow-8X · OB16 · the Tree of Life

44 m³ of water a day

Model result for ten modules running around the clock in La Guajira mean conditions. To be confirmed on an instrumented prototype.

A large-scale atmospheric water generator built from ten refrigerated modules, called BEASTs. Each one cools humid air in two stages to condense its water, and recovers energy from every stream before letting it go: the cold, dry air leaving the machine and the cold water it has just made.

Built for places where water is scarce but the air is not dry: hot, humid coasts, islands and ports.

f. 6v

How it works

  1. Air intake. Each module draws in about 4.5 m³ of hot ambient air per second at the branch tips.
  2. Heat recovery. The incoming air is pre-cooled by the cold, dry air leaving the machine, in a recovery exchanger (RevB2). Some water already condenses here.
  3. Stage 1. Refrigerated plates at about 8 °C, with some 26 m² of cold surface per stage, condense most of the water.
  4. Stage 2. A second, smaller stage finishes drying the air.
  5. Dry air reused. The cold, dry air passes back through the recovery exchanger and then over the refrigeration condenser, lowering its temperature so the compressor uses less electricity. Only then is it released through the crown.
  6. Cold water reused. The condensate leaves at about 8 °C. Before storage it flows through a stratified tank (CTRB) that uses its coldness to help cool the refrigeration circuit at the hottest hours.
  7. Treatment and storage. The water is filtered and treated to drinking-water standard, then stored at the base or sent to a reservoir.

Airflow is adjusted hour by hour. When the air is hot and relatively dry, the machine slows down; when each kilowatt-hour yields more water, it speeds up. The aim is the most water for the least energy, not the most water at any cost.
